I've had many conversations with Chinese companies expanding overseas.

They often start with market questions.

Which country has more opportunity? Which industry is growing faster? Which region has less competition?

But if you keep talking, something interesting happens.

The conversation shifts from markets to people.

Because markets can be figured out.

You can do research. Test and iterate. Build channels gradually. Adjust products over time.

But people? That's different.

Especially in the early days of entering a new market, the first few hires make or break everything.

They might lead marketing. Operations. Supply chain. Or just a small team.

But these early people determine so much:

- How well they understand the local market
- How effectively they communicate with headquarters
- How quickly they build the first customer relationships
- How fast they assemble the initial team

Get these people right, everything accelerates.

Get them wrong, the company spends months—or years—recovering.

* * *

Here's what many Chinese companies ask when going global:

Is there someone who understands the local market AND can work with Chinese teams?

Sounds simple. But in reality, these people are rare.

Some know the local market well but never worked with Chinese companies.

Others understand Chinese company rhythm but lack local resources.

Some have both— but the company can't tell if they're truly trustworthy.

So hiring always comes back to one question:

Can we trust this person?

In cross-border teams, trust isn't built instantly.

It comes from:

- Past projects delivered together
- Reputation in the industry
- Mutual connections
- Shared work history

These scattered dots slowly form a trust network.

And key talent moves through these networks.

Going global is not just a product competition. It's not just a capital competition.

It's a talent competition.

Who finds the right people first.

Who builds stable cross-cultural teams faster.

Who enables people from different backgrounds to work together effectively.

These questions determine long-term global position.

* * *

When we look back at Chinese companies' global expansion years from now, we'll notice something:

Many pivotal moments came down to specific people.

A country manager who made it work.

An early team member who bridged cultures.

Someone who understood both China and the world.

They might not appear in headlines.

But so many stories start with them.

* * *

### If you want to position yourself for this talent war:

This week: Assess your "bridge" value. What connects you to both Chinese companies and your local market?

This month: Research 3 Chinese companies expanding in your region. What gaps are they trying to fill?

This quarter: Build your "cross-cultural credibility." Document projects, connections, and results that demonstrate your ability to bridge worlds.

Ongoing: Stay visible. The war for talent is won by those who can be found.

* * *

Positioning yourself for opportunities with Chinese global companies? Happy to chat 1:1 about:

- Identifying which companies are expanding in your market
- Demonstrating your cross-cultural value
- Building connections that matter
- Navigating the talent landscape

Send me a message if you want to chat.

* * *

In the global expansion game, products and capital matter.

But the real winners are those who find the right people first.

The question isn't where the market is going.

The question is: Who will get there first?
