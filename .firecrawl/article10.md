![De-risking China Supply Chain? The China+1 Strategy Can be a Joke.](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/D5612AQFtZ7vXaMweNg/article-cover_image-shrink_720_1280/B56Z1rFMQ2HoAI-/0/1775618025063?e=2147483647&v=beta&t=SpkZkSc81doNwVhnyK_hMnd6IWCSZnUn5t3Zc0tAEAE)

A supply chain director at a European consumer electronics group told me: "We moved assembly to Malaysia to satisfy the board. Six months later, 70% of our tier-2 suppliers had Chinese ownership.

Everyone is talking about de-risking by moving supply chain out from China. But while your procurement deck says China+1, the data says something else entirely.

China's industrial component exports to Southeast Asia like mobile parts, processors, lithium batteries have surged to record levels.

China's FDI into ASEAN has grown at 11% CAGR over the past decade. You moved production to Vietnam. Indonesia. Thailand. China moved with you.

### 1 \| You did not leave China. You just added a stop.

Vietnam's electronics manufacturing now runs on Chinese components. Processors from Shenzhen. Battery cells from [CATL](https://cn.linkedin.com/company/contemporary-amperex-technology-gmbh?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention)'s partners. Display modules from Guangdong suppliers who opened satellite warehouses in Ho Chi Minh City.

The factory is in Vietnam. The nervous system is still in China.

### 2 \| China's FDI into ASEAN is not charity, it is infrastructure for control.

A supply chain director at a European consumer electronics group told me: "We moved assembly to Malaysia to satisfy the board.

Six months later, 70% of our tier-2 suppliers had Chinese ownership. We had not de-risked. We had just changed our PowerPoint."

### 3 \| The China+1 strategy is real.

The +1 just happens to be funded by China. When global brands started shifting production post-2018, Chinese manufacturers followed. They built or bought into the same industrial zones.

When you source locally in Thailand, you may be buying from a subsidiary of the same Chinese group you were trying to diversify away from.

### 4 \| Component dependency is the new oil dependency, and harder to see.

Oil dependency was visible: tankers, pipelines, chokepoints on a map. Component dependency is invisible until it is not. Lithium battery cells. Rare earth magnets. Advanced processors. Moving final assembly to ASEAN does not change the equation.

### 5 \| The leaders who are winning are not pretending.

They are mapping. The best supply chain operators I know are not trying to eliminate China. They are mapping where the dependency lives, which nodes are substitutable, and building contingency depth around the irreplaceable parts.

Practical Starting Points:

Conduct a tier-2 and tier-3 audit for Chinese ownership

Identify components with no viable non-China source at volume

Build 90-day strategic stockpiles on your three most critical single-source components

Diversification is real strategy.

But the data is telling a clear story: China-ASEAN supply chain is now deeply integrated, partly because of corporate de-risking efforts. China followed the demand.

Your job is to understand exactly what you depend on, and build resilience around that honest picture, not a politically convenient one.

Have you done a tier-2 or tier-3 supplier audit and found more China exposure than expected?

Working with China may not be a problem, your mindset of focusing on not working with China could be a big problem.
