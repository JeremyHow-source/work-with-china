# Before I answer "how to find a job in China," I usually ask this question

- [Report this article](https://www.linkedin.com/uas/login?session_redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.linkedin.com%2Fpulse%2Fbefore-i-answer-how-find-job-china-usually-ask-question-wenchao-wang-cyszc&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_ellipsis-menu-semaphore-sign-in-redirect&guestReportContentType=PONCHO_ARTICLE&_f=guest-reporting)

### Wenchao Wang


Published Feb 28, 2026



[\+ Follow](https://www.linkedin.com/signup/cold-join?session_redirect=%2Fpulse%2Fbefore-i-answer-how-find-job-china-usually-ask-question-wenchao-wang-cyszc&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_publisher-author-card)

In the past year or two, I've been getting more and more messages like these.

Most come from overseas students and professionals interested in China, or people who already have some contact with Chinese companies.

They usually get straight to the point:

"Can you recommend jobs in China?"

Or

"How can I work with Chinese companies?"

I usually don't start by answering about jobs, platforms, or visas.

I ask a simpler question first:

### Why China?

Why not Singapore? Why not Malaysia? Why not Vietnam? Why not stay in your home country and find Asia-related opportunities there?

What's interesting is that many people's first reaction is:

"Actually, other countries would work too."

This one sentence says a lot.

It shows they haven't really thought through "Why Work with China." They just have a vague sense that "Asia has opportunities," "China is growing fast," "I hear there are lots of opportunities."

This is trend perception, not strategic choice.

Among the international talent I've worked with over the years, those who truly commit to long-term China-related careers almost never make decisions casually.

They usually start by thinking through a deeper question:

Am I looking for a short-term job location, or am I choosing my position in a long-term opportunity network?

If you're also thinking about whether to Work with China, I suggest you take some time to answer a few questions first.

1 List 3 countries or regions in Asia you're interested in. For each, write down specifically "why."

2 Then ask yourself: if this reason didn't exist, would I still choose it?

3 Talk to 2 people who are already there. See what their real experience is like.

Understanding "why" matters more than understanding "how."

Because direction is more important than speed.

Beyond asking “Why China?”, it means actively networking, doing serious research, and learning how Chinese companies actually operate.

It’s not enough to reach out to one or two people and hope that a single call will unlock everything.
