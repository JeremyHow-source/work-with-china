# 20 Questions to Answer Before You Pursue a Job at a Chinese Company

**Author:** Wenchao Wang  
**Published:** August 5, 2026

---

I have had this conversation more times than I can count.

People messaged me saying they are interested in Chinese companies. They have done some research. They follow the news. They might even have a target list. But they have not moved yet.

They are waiting for things become clear before taking actions. But they are not very sure what to become clear or even how to define "clear".

The problem is actuallly lack of clarity.

So I built a list. Twenty questions, two groups. I use them with everyone I talked to, and I am sharing them here because I think most people asking about this path are asking the wrong question.

They ask, "How do I get in?" when the first question is, "Have I been honest with myself?"

The twenty questions are from Chapter 16 of my new book *Work With China: From Zero to Offer*.

It is a step-by-step job search playbook: how to identify the right type of Chinese company, build a targeted list, approach decision-makers directly, position a resume for a Chinese HR audience, navigate a Chinese-style interview, and negotiate a compensation package that accounts for housing allowance, tax structure, and visa costs.

---

### GROUP ONE: Have you actually thought it through?

These first ten are for the person who is still asking "do I really want this?" Not everyone needs them. Some people have been clear for years. But most people are more undecided than they admit.

* **Question 1:** How long have you felt drawn to Chinese companies? Did that feeling start with an article last week, or have you been sitting with it for years? There is a difference, and you know which one it is.
* **Question 2:** If none of your friends understood your choice, would you still make it? This is the first real filter. A lot of interest in China careers is half-borrowed from someone else's idea of an interesting life.
* **Question 3:** What can you bring that a Chinese hire cannot? Not in the abstract. Specifically. If you cannot answer this with one clear sentence, you are not ready to pitch yourself to anyone.
* **Question 4:** Are you willing to spend your first year or two genuinely learning a system that does not always make sense to you, without demanding that it change first?
* **Question 5:** Does your family know? Have you had a real conversation, not just mentioned it once? And if you had no income for the next three to six months, could your finances hold?
* **Question 6:** If you go, and three months in you realize it is not right, can you accept that without calling it a failure? How you define failure shapes your result more than the choice itself.
* **Question 7:** How much overtime can you genuinely accept, not as a performance, but as a lifestyle?
* **Question 8:** Have you spent real time with one specific Chinese company? Not the brand. The actual people. Who do they hire? What backgrounds do their foreign employees have? Where are those employees now?
* **Question 9:** Are you someone who finds their rhythm quickly in unfamiliar places, or do you need a long runway?
* **Question 10:** Five years from now, where do you want to be, and with whom, doing what? Do not rush this one. If the answer is not clear, that is an answer too.

---

### GROUP TWO: Are you ready to move?

These next ten are for the person who has already decided and just needs to know where they stand.

* **Question 11:** Can you finish this sentence? "I am a professional in X, and I help target employers reach Y result." If you cannot, you do not have a positioning statement. You have a job title.
* **Question 12:** Does your resume have a W-version for Western companies and a C-version for Chinese ones? Is the opening paragraph written for a Chinese employer, or are you still sending the same version to everyone?
* **Question 13:** Does your LinkedIn headline include your role, your industry, your region, and the most credible company or platform name worth mentioning?
* **Question 14:** Have you narrowed your T1 list to seven to ten specific companies? Have you looked up who their foreign employees are right now?
* **Question 15:** Do you have an outreach plan for each T1 company? Not a template. A plan. Which channel, which person, what specifically you are bringing to that conversation.
* **Question 16:** Have you written your cross-cultural story? One real moment, in 100 to 150 words, where two cultures collided and you learned something. This is what makes you a person, not a resume.
* **Question 17:** Have you set a follow-up rule? Four weeks of silence means follow up again. Eight weeks means keep pushing and open a parallel track.
* **Question 18:** Do you have one person who knows what you are doing, someone you can message when you feel low? This sounds soft. It is not. The people who drop out are usually the ones doing this alone.
* **Question 19:** Have you written your execution targets and put them somewhere visible every day? 100 applications. 3 interviews. 5 to 10 real connections. Numbers, not intentions.
* **Question 20:** What is the one thing you can do today?

---

### Final Thoughts

Most of these questions do not have a perfect answer. That is not the point.

The point is the "no" column. Every no next to a question is a specific gap, and a specific gap is easier to close than a vague sense of not being ready.

There are people with almost everything in order convince themselves they are not ready. There are also people with obvious gaps move anyway, and figure it out as they went. The ones who moved almost always got somewhere. The ones who waited for certainty almost always found a new reason to keep waiting.

There is a line I keep coming back to, from *The Courage to Be Disliked*. The problem is not that you do not know what to do. The problem is that you are not yet willing to give up what is comfortable in order to change.

That is not a character flaw. It is just how people work. But it is worth naming honestly.

Go through the list. Write your answers on paper, not in your head. Then look at question 20, and do that one thing today.
