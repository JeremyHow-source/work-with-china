## Why So Many Global Professionals Are Curious About "Working With China"

Around November last year, I started writing a small content series on LinkedIn called “Work with China.”

At the beginning, it was simply something I wanted to try. I had spent 20 years working for global companies, with Chinese companies and global teams, and I often felt there were many misunderstandings, but also many opportunities that people didn’t see clearly.

So I started writing.

Yesterday my LinkedIn followers just crossed 5,000.

For many people this may not sound like a big number. But for me, it felt quite meaningful.

One thing that surprised me personally was the speed of this growth.

The first 2,500 people in my network actually took me more than 15 years to accumulate through work, projects, conferences, and collaborations around the world.

But the next 2,500 followers came within just four months after I started writing the Work with China series.

For me, that contrast says something quite interesting.

First, the topic of working with China has clearly become something many global professionals are thinking about. It resonates with people across different industries and countries.

Second, it also showed me that when you openly share your own experience, insights, and observations, the content can genuinely create value for others.

For me, this number is less about the milestone itself.

It’s more about the people behind it.

Over the past few months, many of you sent messages, added me, or shared your thoughts after reading the articles.

Some told me the posts helped them understand how Chinese companies operate.

Some said the ideas resonated with their own work experience.

Others simply said they enjoyed the perspective.

These small messages meant a lot to me.

They are also the reason I want to keep writing this series.

* * *

### 28 Conversations Later

During these four months, manypeople reached out and I had conversations with 28 of them.

Originally I kept calls completely open and free, simply because I was curious to understand the real questions and challenges global professionals have about working with China.

Many of these conversations were thoughtful and honest.

But one pattern also became very clear.

More than half of the people were hoping that somehow I could help them find a job connected to China.

And that made me realize something important.

For many people, the idea of “working with China” is not always driven by real interest in China.

Sometimes it is simply because they are looking for a job.

Or they believe China has many opportunities.

But before making that choice, many people haven’t really thought through a few key questions:

What are the other options available to them?

What are the pros and cons of choosing China?

What resources they already have around them?

And how they can actually use those resources.

* * *

### Not Everyone Is in the Same Position

In my view, people interested in working with China usually fall into four broad groups.

Chinese professionals with a global perspective who are based in China.

Chinese professionals who are living overseas, but still have strong international exposure.

Global professionals who are already working in China.

And global professionals who want to work with Chinese companies from overseas.

Each group has very different advantages.

Different language abilities.

Different cultural understanding.

Different networks.

And different ways of approaching opportunities.

For example, some people may be better suited to work for Chinese companies expanding globally.

Others may be more suited to international companies operating in China.

But this kind of reflection is often missing when people first start exploring this path.

* * *

### Where Many Opportunities Actually Are

Another thing I noticed is how people search for jobs.

Many rely mainly on platforms like LinkedIn, Indeed, or Glassdoor.

These platforms are useful.

But when it comes to Chinese companies, they are not always the main place where opportunities appear.

Many Chinese companies, especially mid-size ones, do not actively use international recruitment platforms.

Some of them don’t even have a LinkedIn page.

Instead, a lot of positions are simply listed on their own company websites.

In a way, this creates an interesing situation: there is certian information gap.

But for people who know where to look, it can actually mean less competition.

### Opportunities Often Come From Conversations

Another pattern I saw is that many opportunities appear through conversations rather than job postings.

Talking with people who have worked in China.

Connecting with Chinese friends.

Or meeting professionals who already collaborate with Chinese companies.

Sometimes a simple conversation opens a door that you didn’t even know existed.

Industry events are another example.

Many Chinese companies participate in international trade fairs or exhibitions.

Often their founders or senior executives attend these events themselves.

When people meet them there, build a first impression, and start a relationship, it sometimes leads to opportunities later.

Not always immediately.

But relationships accumulate over time.

* * *

### What I Plan to Build Next

These conversations also made me think about how this work could create more value.

So I recently started to build a Work with China Network.

The idea is simple.

A professional community where global professionals connect and engage with Chinese companies.

There are three types of people who may find this network meaningful.

Global professionals who used to work or working inside of Chinese companies.

Global professionals who have business relationships with Chinese companies, such as consultants, advisors, partners, clients, or suppliers.

Global professionals who are simply curious about working with Chinese companies and want to explore that possibility.

Inside the network, we will focus on a few things.

Cross-cultural trust.

Relationships.

Connections.

And opportunities.

Every month we will host one online session, where experienced global professionals share their experience working with China.

Sometimes we may also invite leaders from Chinese companies to share their perspectives.

The goal is to help people understand China in a more practical and balanced way.

PM me if you are interested to be part of the first 50 members.

* * *

### Make It Happen

Another thing that became clear from the conversations is that many people need more structured guidance when thinking about their China-related career path.

So I will also start offering a career consultation to global professionals focused on working with Chinese companies.

This is not mainly about sending job opportunities.

Instead, the conversation usually starts with more strategic questions.

What could a career path connected to China look like over the next 3, 5, or 10 years?

Together we might explore questions like:

Whether working with China is actually the right direction.

Which industries might make sense.

What type of Chinese companies could be a better fit.

And how someone’s own background and experience could translate in that context.

Alongside this, there will also be a few more specific services.

Helping people rewrite a Chinese-focused CV.

Preparing for interviews with Chinese companies.

And doing basic due diligence on potential employers before someone accepts an offer.

* * *

### A Simple Thank You

Looking back, the past few months have been an interesting journey.

It started with a few posts.

Then a series.

Then conversations with people from different countries who are all trying to understand one question:

What does it really mean to work with China today?

The messages, encouragement, and conversations from many of you are the reason I keep writing.

So thank you for reading.

And thank you for the conversations along the way.

The series will continue.

And I’m curious to hear your thoughts.
